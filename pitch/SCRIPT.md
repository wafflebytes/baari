## The first hundred homes, in full

N3 shows the short version. This is the version for questions, and for us.

The YC rule is Paul Graham's "do things that don't scale": recruit users by hand, one at a time, and treat the first ones as if they were the only ones. Airbnb's founders photographed hosts' apartments themselves. The Collisons installed Stripe on a new user's laptop on the spot.

### What the first draft got wrong

I grilled the first version of this plan. Six holes, and what replaced each:

1. **"The fourteen families we interviewed" aren't our users.** Of the six transcripts in `research/`, three say "no cook" outright, one has a maid, and in the rest the mother cooks. They told us the problem. They aren't homes where a part-time cook needs a brief every morning. Replaced by our own homes and our neighbours'.
2. **Baari runs one household today.** Rails holds the synthetic Sharma family. Households keyed by id, a run lock per household and a 7-day expiry are designed (`prd/FINALE_HANDOFF.md` section 6) and not built. Nothing below starts until they are. This is the first build after the finale.
3. **There's no real money yet.** Pine Labs is on the sandbox, and going live needs a merchant account, which needs a company. So for the first hundred, Baari plans, briefs the cook and sends Sharma Kirana the order, and the family pays the kirana by UPI as they do today. Baari's own payments switch on when a real mandate does. That's why "help us take our first hundred homes from sandbox to real money" is in the ask.
4. **Parents live on WhatsApp, not Telegram.** Asking a 55-year-old to install a new app is the biggest drop-off in the plan. For a hundred homes we fix it by hand: we install Telegram on the parents' phones during setup and send their first message with them. WhatsApp is what makes the next thousand possible, and it isn't built.
5. **DMing fifty-four strangers from a reel is spam.** Most comments on a reel like that are tags and laughing emojis, not raised hands. Replying from a brand-new account gets flagged. The one person worth writing to is the creator: she has a cook, she has the problem on camera, and she has 114,000 viewers. If she tries it and posts again, that's worth more than every comment. Expect a handful of homes from the reel, not dozens.
6. **"Win one cook, get her route" ignores the cook.** A cook may not want her employers comparing notes, about pay or anything else. So the cook doesn't recruit. Neighbours do. In a housing society, flats already share cooks and recommend them in the society's WhatsApp group. The cook's part is to say, unprompted, that one house now sends her a clear brief and has the groceries paid at the kirana before she arrives. She benefits, so she mentions it. We never ask her to sell.

### The plan, in four stages

| Stage | Homes | Weeks after the finale | How |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Build | 0 | 1 to 2 | Households on rails, one run lock per household, cost per night measured on our own homes. No users yet |
| 1. Our own | 10 | 3 to 4 | Chaitanya's home (a cook for three years), Vinay's and Keshav's if they have cooks `[CHECK]`, then neighbours in Chaitanya's society. Set up in person, in the kitchen, with the cook there for her first voice note |
| 2. One society | 30 | 5 to 8 | The society we started in. A notice in its WhatsApp group from a resident who already uses it, never from us. A weekend table at the society gate: "bring your phone, ten minutes" |
| 3. Three more | 100 | 9 to 12 | Societies where a stage-2 cook also works, or where a stage-2 family's relatives live. Plus the creator, and anyone the reel brings |

The gate between stages: at least six of the first ten homes still run a night in week three, without us reminding them. If they don't, we stop recruiting and fix what made them stop. Ten homes we can call one by one. A hundred unhappy homes we can't.

A human reads every night. For the first 30 days, one of us reads every home's DECISIONS log the next morning and calls the house when something looks wrong. At about two minutes a log, a hundred homes is a bit over three hours a day, split across three of us. That's how we find the eleventh bad night. After 30 days, we read a sample.

### What we measure

From `prd/PRODUCT.md` section 10, and all three are already countable from rails logs:
- agreed-and-eaten days out of cooking days
- time to lock, from shortlist to locked dish
- the share of nights someone other than the usual person held the baari

The one we'd put on a wall: someone other than Mummy holds the baari three nights a week, in week four.

### What we don't know yet

- **Cost per home per night.** About twelve agent runs a night on GPT-5.4. We haven't measured what that costs, and a hundred homes is twelve hundred runs a night.
- **Whether AgenticOrg is where a hundred homes should run.** It's a hackathon platform, and we don't know its limits on concurrent runs (T22 in the test plan). We may have to run the agent ourselves.
- **Price.** Nobody's paid us anything. Stage 1 is free. We'll ask stage-2 homes what they'd pay before we set a number.
