# Keshav's demo voice

Keshav is the person on the team who asks the questions. He talks to households, writes down what they said word for word, and comes back with the thing nobody expected. On stage he's the calm one between Chaitanya's opinions and Vinay's numbers. His job is to make the jury believe the problem is real because real people said it, not because we did.

This file was written without a recording of Keshav. It's a starting point built from his role on the team and the tone of Chaitanya's and Vinay's files. When Keshav reads his lines out loud and something doesn't sound like him, he changes the line and this file both.

## What the voice is

- **Start with what someone said.** A quote, then what it means. "One mother told us, in Marathi, ninety percent of it is handled in the mind." He lets the family make the argument.
- **Admit what we got wrong.** "We walked in thinking this was a recipe problem. It isn't." Being wrong first is his credibility. Chaitanya has opinions; Keshav has corrections.
- **Numbers he can point to.** Eleven households before we built, three after. 299 minutes and 97. He never rounds up and never says "many families".
- **Dry, a little deadpan.** He's funny by understatement, not by jokes. "They all said kuch bhi." Then a pause and nothing else.
- **Warm about the people.** Mummy, the cook, the son who ate out: he describes them the way you'd describe your own family, never as "users" or "personas".
- **Hindi where the family would use it.** "Kuch bhi", "Papa ki thali mein aloo nahi", "teri baari". Quoted, not translated, two or three a section.

## What to strip

- Research jargon: "pain points", "insights", "user journey", "qualitative data", "we synthesised". Say what they said.
- Hedging: "it seemed like", "a lot of people kind of". Say "seven of eleven" or say nothing.
- Stacking quotes. One quote per point. The best one.
- Explaining the method before the finding. Nobody on the jury wants the interview guide.

## Rhythm rules

- Short sentences, 5 to 15 words. He speaks a little slower than Chaitanya, so he gets fewer words per slot.
- Quote, pause, meaning. Mark the pause with `(pause)` after every quote.
- One remembered line per section, at the end. Often it's the family's words, not his.
- Mark slides with `[SHOW: ...]` so the script is also the run sheet.

## On payments and growth

Keshav also carries the money story and the first hundred homes. He does it the same way: from the family's side.

- Money is permission, not a feature. "A plan that can't buy the tomatoes is one more menu on the fridge door."
- Say the limit in rupees, as the parent would hear it. "Ask above three hundred. Never more than four hundred a day."
- Never frame overspend as the cook's fault. It's budget drift that both sides see.
- Growth is people he'll actually talk to: the fourteen families, the reel's comments, the cook's other houses. Never "acquisition channels".
- Technical Pine Labs questions go to Vinay. Keshav says "Vinay built that" and hands over without apologising.

## Avoid

- Hype words: revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, leverage, unlock, empower.
- Claiming the research is bigger than it is. Fourteen households is a strong start, not a study.
- Anything that isn't built, unless he says "next".
