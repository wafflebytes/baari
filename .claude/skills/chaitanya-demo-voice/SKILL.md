# Chaitanya's Demo Voice

This skill turns ideas into lines Chaitanya can say out loud and sound like himself. He talks naturally: grounded, opinionated and concrete. The demo version is that same person after a night's sleep and three rehearsals. Use the same voice, cut the wandering, and give every line a job.

The goal is to sound like him, not to copy his words. Use his instincts and rhythm, and pick a sharper word whenever one fits better. Never force his exact phrases into the script.

## What to keep (this is the voice)

- **Start with a real moment, then explain it.** He doesn't open with "the market for X is huge." He opens with a scene people recognize ("aaj kya banega?"), then explains why it matters.
- **Talk about real products by name.** He backs his opinions with things he actually uses (Claude Code from his phone, an assistant on WhatsApp). Judges trust specifics.
- **Strong, simple opinions.** "Why would it bother you always?" "Don't shove your agent at me, let me bring mine." Say it plainly and move on.
- **Respect for the user.** His recurring theme is that the product should come to the user, show up only when needed, and stay out of the way otherwise. Let that run through the whole pitch.
- **Warm and conversational.** He talks *to* people, not *at* them. An occasional rhetorical question or check-in is fine.
- **A light Indian, everyday flavor.** A few Hindi phrases make the problem real and memorable: "aaj kya banega", "char rajma baaki hai", kirana. Use 2–3 per demo, as hooks. Don't scatter them everywhere.
- **Plain, confident vocabulary.** Words like "always on", "lives where you are", "gets out of your way", "trust", "overhead", "switching cost". A business term is fine when it carries real meaning, like *switching cost* or *engagement loop*. Never use one for decoration.

## What to strip (spoken habits that don't survive a demo)

- Filler: "you know", "something like that", "I would say", "basically", "so yeah", "okay cool", "whatever it might be".
- **"right?" after most sentences.** Keep at most one per section, only where it lands as a genuine question.
- Mid-sentence self-corrections ("it's an intelligent being... I should not call it a being"). Write the corrected version.
- Rambling. He tends to stack 4–5 examples in one breath. Pick the best one or two and drop the rest.
- Hedging ("I think this might be the perfect example"). Say it with conviction: "This is what a personal agent should look like in 2026."

## Rhythm rules

- Short spoken sentences, mostly 6–18 words. One idea each.
- Stack ideas in threes at most.
- Give each section one line written to be remembered, and put it at the end of the section.
- Mark pauses with `(pause)` and on-screen moments with `[SHOW: ...]`, so the script doubles as a run sheet.
- Read every line out loud in your head. If it takes two breaths, split it.

## Avoid

- Hype words: revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, leverage, synergy, cutting-edge.
- Explaining AI basics to CEOs. Assume they're smart and busy.
- Talking about the tech stack before the user's problem.
- Over-claiming. If something is planned rather than built, say "next" or "we're building toward".

## Pitch skeleton (default order)

1. **The moment.** A scene everyone in the room has lived (10–15s).
2. **Why it hurts.** The hidden overhead behind that moment (15s).
3. **What we built.** One sentence: what it is and where it lives.
4. **Why it's different.** The ambient part: it comes to you, only when needed.
5. **The demo.** Two or three concrete moments, each ending in a visible payoff.
6. **Why it wins.** Memory, then trust, then switching cost.
7. **The closing line.** A vision statement in plain words.

## Bari: facts to draw from (from Chaitanya, Oct 2026)

- Problem: in Indian households with a cook, someone has to answer "aaj kya banega?" every day. Behind that question sits planning fatigue, limited ideas, inventory tracking, and handing the cook money for groceries.
- Bari is an always-on ambient agent that lives in the household's Telegram or WhatsApp group. A companion app exists so people can see and feel the product, but nobody has to open it.
- It speaks up only when it needs something it doesn't have, or when spending goes over. Otherwise it stays quiet.
- It remembers the household: spending patterns, how often they eat out, food preferences, family members, how the cook handles different situations.
- It can spend, and it saves the kirana's number without bothering anyone.
- It understands Indian cooking context, for example that rajma needs soaking the day before.
- Anyone, including the cook, can just say "char rajma baaki hai" and Bari keeps track. No logging and no tracker to maintain.
- Moat: household memory builds up over time, so switching away gets expensive.
- Vision: this is what a personal AI agent should be in 2026. It isn't a planner you go back to and fill in. It quietly runs part of your home.

## Calibration: raw vs. demo voice

**Raw (how he says it):**
> So we are here to pitch Bari which is an always on ambient agent that lives basically invisibly right in your household I would say so why do I say invisibly is because it does have a companion app where you can you know touch the buttons... but it does not really need you to go to the app right so it can live where you are...

**Demo voice:**
> This is Bari. It's an always-on agent that lives inside your household's WhatsApp group.
> There's an app, if you want to see what it's doing. But you never have to open it.
> Bari comes to you. And only when it actually needs something. (pause)
> Because why should it bother you otherwise?

**Raw:**
> the problem today with Indian households in general is aaj kya banega right it's like basically we are assuming that almost all of the segments that we are targeting has a cook...

**Demo voice:**
> Every household in this room with a cook hears one question every single day.
> *Aaj kya banega?* (pause)
> It sounds small. But behind it is planning, stock-checking, handing over cash, and running out of ideas. Every day.

## Sensitive moments: wording that protects trust

- Never describe overspend as an "integrity issue" or anything that blames the cook. Frame it as **budget drift**, with both the owner and the cook seeing the same numbers.
- Overspend alerts are opt-in and quiet: never on by default, never a push notification, never marketed as a selling point.
- When explaining when Bari speaks up, use the **cost of inaction** frame: "Bari weighs what changed, what it costs to stay quiet, and how confident it is. If it's not sure, it asks. Anything involving money needs a confirmation."

## Live Q&A voice

- Don't open with a hedge like "I don't have a very good answer." Open with "Here's how I think about it," then give the answer.
- Answer first in one line, then add one supporting reason. Stop there, without stacking examples.
- If something isn't built yet, say "that's next" plainly. Don't apologize for it.

## Final check before handing over a script

- [ ] Could Chaitanya say every line without sounding like he's reading?
- [ ] Is there one quotable line per section?
- [ ] Is the filler gone, while the warmth and opinions stay?
- [ ] Does it fit the slot length when read at about 140 words per minute?
- [ ] Is every claim something Bari actually does today, or clearly marked as "next"?
