# Unslop

Edit text to remove AI patterns and add human voice.

## Process

1. Scan for the patterns below.
2. Rewrite. Preserve meaning, match intended tone.
3. Add soul (see next section).
4. Self-audit: "What makes this obviously AI generated?" Fix remaining tells.

## Adding soul

Removing patterns is half the job. Sterile, voiceless writing is just as obvious.

- **Have opinions.** React to facts instead of neutrally listing pros and cons.
- **Vary rhythm.** Short sentences. Then longer ones that take their time. Mix it up.
- **Acknowledge complexity.** "Impressive but also kind of unsettling" beats "impressive."
- **Use "I" when it fits.** First person isn't unprofessional.
- **Let some mess in.** Perfect structure looks machine-made.
- **Be specific.** Not "this is concerning" but "there's something unsettling about agents churning away at 3am."

## Patterns to detect and fix

### Content

1. **Puffery.** "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape", "setting the stage for", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted". Cut puffery, state what happened.
2. **Name-dropping.** Listing media outlets without context. Pick one, say what was said.
3. **Superficial -ing phrases.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting...", "showcasing...", "fostering...". Delete or expand with real sources.
4. **Promotional language.** "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking", "groundbreaking", "renowned", "stunning", "must-visit". Use neutral descriptions.
5. **Vague attributions.** "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest", "Some critics argue". Name the source or delete.
6. **Formulaic challenges.** "Despite challenges... continues to thrive." Replace with specific facts.

### Language

7. **AI vocabulary.** Additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape (abstract), pivotal, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore, vibrant. Replace with plain words.
8. **Fancy ways to say "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Just say "is" or "has".
9. **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly instead.
10. **Rule of three.** Forcing ideas into groups of three. Use the natural number.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Protagonist, main character, central figure, hero all in one paragraph. Pick one, repeat it.
12. **False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y aren't on a meaningful scale. List topics directly.

## assistant  2026-09-10T17:44:31.983Z

Read it, plus the four transcripts, since the ideas should come off the raw material rather than the doc's own framing.

Two things in the transcripts change what "agentic" should mean here, and neither is in the synthesis yet:

**Everyone buys first and cooks after.** Respondent 4: "we have the groceries first and then we decide what to eat later." Respondent 6: "they order the things whatever available in market, and then they cook according to that." Respondent 7 and 8 say the same. Every meal-planning product on earth is built menu-first, then generates a shopping list. That is backwards for these households. The planning unit is the pantry, not the menu.

**The market run is already a voice loop.** Respondent 8: "if the fridge feels empty, the first call goes to my father, like what to bring from the market." That is a human agent making a procurement decision over a phone call with incomplete state. It fails constantly. It is also the single most natural place to put Gnani and Pine Labs in the same loop.

And the test I'm applying to every idea below: an agent earns the name if it maintains state nobody typed in, acts under uncertainty with real consequences (a call placed, money moved), and repairs itself when reality contradicts its plan. A cron job that calls an LLM and sends a WhatsApp card is not an agent, and a judge who builds things will see through it in about four seconds.

## 1. Cook Ki Antaratma, the morning call that negotiates instead of asking

The 7:45am outbound Gnani call, but the agent's job is not to read out a plan. It is to extract true state from a person whose social incentive is to agree with you. Your own synthesis already names this ("cooks say haan to please"), then treats it as a UI problem solved by repeat-back. It is actually the hardest agentic problem in the product.

An agent that handles it has to design its own questions per call. Never ask "hai kya?" because the answer is always haan. Ask for a count ("kitne tamatar dikh rahe hain?"). Ask her to name what she sees rather than confirm what you claim. Occasionally plant a wrong item in the repeat-back and see if she corrects it, which gives you a live calibration score for how much to trust that cook's yeses. That score then feeds every downstream decision, including how much money you let her spend unsupervised. Over ten calls the agent learns that Sunita corrects you and Rekha never does, and it asks them different questions.

Gnani is load-bearing three ways: sub-500ms so the pause before an answer is diagnostic rather than network lag, barge-in so you can interrupt a reflexive haan mid-word, and code-switching because she will answer "do kilo aata hai but sabzi khatam" in one breath.

Pine Labs enters mid-call. If she reports paneer missing, the agent does not hang up and send someone a notification. It mints a spend grant and tells her to pick it up on the way, while she is still on the line. That is the demo moment.

## 2. Tijori, the household treasury that issues and revokes grants on its own

This is where Pine Labs stops being a checkout button and becomes the interesting part of the build.

One ReservePay block held by the household. Grantex scopes minted by the agent, not by a human tapping approve: at 7:48am the agent issues a ₹300 scope to the cook, valid 90 minutes, restricted to the two kiranas in the lane, and revokes it at 9:15 whether or not she used it. The dad going to the Sunday market gets a bigger scope with a wider merchant list. The teenager gets ₹150 that only works on the milk booth.

Why an agent and not a rules engine: the grant amount is a decision under uncertainty. It depends on the cook's calibration score from idea 1, what the pantry model thinks is actually missing, how much of the 90-day block is left, and whether the last three retries burned the 3-in-24-hours allowance. The agent has to reason about its own budget over a rolling window, and decide when to ask a human versus when to just act.

Voice biometrics belongs here, and it answers your open question cleanly. The cook has no app and often no smartphone. Her voiceprint from the Gnani call is the authorization factor. She authorizes a household payment by speaking, on a call she was already on, with no device of her own. That is the sentence that makes Pine Labs lean in, because it generalizes instantly to drivers, maids, nannies, caretakers, every domestic worker in India who currently gets handed a ₹500 note and no receipt.

## 3. Sauda, the agent that runs the market call

Intercept the call that already happens. When the pantry model drops below what tomorrow needs, the agent calls whoever is going out, in their language, and gives them a list that is already reconciled against what is at home, what got cooked yesterday, and what nobody in the house will eat. It negotiates on the phone: "bhindi ₹40 hai to le lena, ₹80 hai to chhod dena." It pays through the Pine Labs grant when they reach the counter.

This one matters because it works in Respondent 8's village where there is no Blinkit, and in Respondent 7's town where dad brings groceries home from the shop. Any design that assumes quick commerce is a metro-only product. Yours does not have to be.

Agentic content: the agent has to decide what to put on the list under real uncertainty about what is in the house, and it has to handle a human who comes back with substitutions. Palak was not available so I got methi. That single sentence has to rewrite tomorrow's plan, the pantry model, and possibly the duty-holder's card, without anyone touching an app.

## 4. Rasoi ki yaad, the pantry model that estimates instead of asking

Nobody will ever log consumption. So the agent infers it. It starts from a purchase event (a kirana bill photo, the Blinkit webhook, the Sauda call), decays each item by what the cook confirmed cooking and how many people ate, and maintains a confidence per item. Then it does active learning: on tomorrow's call it asks about the three items where its uncertainty is highest and the cost of being wrong is largest. Not a stock take. Three questions.

This is the piece that makes everything else honest. Without it, the 9pm card is guessing, the grant amounts are guessing, and the market list is guessing. With it, you have a live household state that no competitor has, built from voice and receipts rather than from asking a tired person to type inventory into a form.

Waste, by the way, is not spoilage in your data. Respondent 6: "not because it was bad, it was because nobody eat it." Respondent 7: too much rice at night. The waste is cooked food, and the fix is portion, not shelf life. So the agent's output to the cook includes quantity, and it adjusts quantity from what came back.

## 5. Panchayat, the conflict scheduler

Respondent 4 gave you the cleanest problem in the whole set: dad does not like egg bhurji, he and his brother do, so mom cooks two things or someone gets overruled. Respondent 6's siblings are quietly sick of seasonal vegetables and nobody escalates because it is not worth a fight.

An agent that holds per-member likes, vetoes and health constraints, and resolves them across days rather than within a single meal. Not voting. Scheduling with fairness memory: it knows the siblings have eaten lauki four times in nine days and that dad's preference has won three nights running, and it proposes a week where nobody has to argue because the conflict is already spread out. When it cannot resolve, it says so out loud, which is a feature, because being seen is most of the payload here.

This is a constraint solver with a memory of who has been overruled. It is also the agent that gives the kids and elders a verb, which fixes the "two-person product in a family costume" problem from the audit better than any card does.

## 6. Baari itself, the duty router

Your core mechanic, but the agentic part is not the 9pm card. It is the repair loop. The plan gets invalidated between 9pm and 7:45am constantly: the cook does not show, the paneer is not there, a kid gets sick, guests arrive. The agent's real skill is deciding, per disruption, whether to silently re-solve, tell the duty-holder, or escalate to the other adult. An agent that pings the family for every change is worse than no agent. The escalation policy is the product.

Also worth being honest in the pitch: Respondent 6's mother holds all the state and asks the family, who refuse to decide ("you make anything that you want"). Rotation does not fix that household. Load visibility does. Say that on stage before a judge says it for you.

## 7. Agentic commerce, and why the Pine Labs bend is not quick commerce

You asked why the bend is not built around quick-commerce integration. Because Pine Labs cannot grant it. A Blinkit or Zepto integration is a business development ask aimed at the wrong company, and it is what most teams will pitch, so it also scores zero on originality. Quick commerce is transport. The bend should be about who is allowed to spend, which is the layer Pine Labs actually owns.

The agentic commerce version that does belong: your agent as a machine buyer. It receives an HTTP 402 with a machine-readable payment request from a merchant, checks it against household policy (cap, merchant category, is this already in the pantry, did we buy it yesterday), and pays or declines with no human in the loop. The interesting failure cases are all yours to design: an agent that dedupes against its own pantry model refuses a duplicate order that a human would have placed. That is a demo where the agent visibly saves money by not buying something.

And the delegation ask stands, sharpened by the transcripts: a cook working four flats needs to be a delegate of four primaries at once, which the current model forbids by data model, not policy.

## What I'd actually build

Three agents, one loop, in this order: Rasoi ki yaad, Cook Ki Antaratma, Tijori. That gives you a live state model, a voice interface that extracts truth from a person who agrees with everything, and money that moves on the agent's own decision with a voiceprint as the credential. Both partner rails are load-bearing rather than bolted on, and you can demo the whole thing end to end in ninety seconds with one phone call.

Panchayat and Baari are the strategy story and the naming, and they are cheap to add on top once the state model exists. Sauda is the answer to "does this work outside metros," which someone will ask.

The engineering framing you asked for, briefly. The graph is one typed household object with people, items, dishes and events as nodes, and every write carries a source (call, receipt, webhook, inference) plus a confidence. The context each agent gets is a slice of that graph, not the whole thing: the call agent sees only the three highest-uncertainty items and this cook's calibration score, which is what keeps latency under Gnani's budget. The loop closes in fourteen hours because the 7:45am call corrects the 9pm card of the same day, and that is the actual claim to make about why this stays true where a Sunday plan rots by Wednesday.
